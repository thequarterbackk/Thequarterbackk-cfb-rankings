How the model works

1. Results, not reputation

Only this season’s completed scores enter the model. No polls, recruiting rankings, budgets, conference bonuses, sportsbook lines, or prior-season ratings. Every team begins with the same prior. Conference labels are used only to group the visuals.

2. Schedule-adjusted résumé

The Colley matrix solves wins, losses and opponent strength together across the game network. This avoids banking 100 points for every extra game. Its score ignores scoring margin, so running up a score cannot inflate this component.

3. Opponent-adjusted scoring

A regularized least-squares model measures scoring margins against the strength of each opponent. Margins use 35 × tanh(margin / 35), so blowout credit approaches a limit of 35. Home advantage is estimated jointly from current-season results and shrunk toward zero. All teams receive identical regularization. FCS opponents stay hidden and are measured through direct FBS games.

4. Game-time rank penalties

Every loss costs 50 + 2 × max(0, opponent rank − team rank) + 2 × losing margin. Both model ranks are frozen before that week’s games; tied ratings share the same entering rank. Later results do not rewrite a penalty. Games without prior ranking evidence have no rank-gap term. Hidden non-FBS opponents use FBS count + 1.

Final Rating = 1000 × Colley résumé + 10 × robust scoring power − frozen loss penalties

These blend and penalty weights are explicit policy choices, not fitted probabilities. The entire season is replayed under this version. Historical ranks can differ from earlier model versions. There is no team-specific adjustment.

5. Prediction validation

Forecasts are separate from résumé rankings. A fixed grid of shrinkage and margin limits is tested sequentially: each week trains on earlier weeks only, and settings are chosen using only earlier validation results. A candidate replaces the previous forecasting engine only after at least 100 held-out FBS matchups and at least 1% lower walk-forward mean absolute error, with a positive lower bound on an approximate paired 95% improvement interval. The last week’s outcomes cannot select its own settings. This is a retrospective backtest, distinct from predictions archived before kickoff.

6. Honest limits

No model is free of assumptions. A one-season backtest does not establish national superiority, and forecast accuracy does not prove a résumé ranking is correct. Sparse schedules, disconnected groups and noisy final scores create uncertainty. The source, weights, loss audits and validation figures are public.

7. Live results and ownership

Rankings refresh after final scores. The conference ladder averages published team ratings. The results heatmap shows actual FBS matchups. On the ownership map, the winner takes the losing team’s existing territory. API credentials never enter public files.
